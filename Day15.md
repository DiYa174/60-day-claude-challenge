DAY 15

Learnings:

Complex reports require structured data collection, multi-step reasoning, and organized presentation. Today you'll learn how Claude can transform raw inputs into comprehensive reports that combine analysis, forecasting, and actionable recommendations.

Structured Inputs: Learn how information quality impacts output quality.

Multi-Step Reasoning: Generate reports through layered analysis.

Report Generation: Create detailed and organized outputs.

Prompt Design: Build prompts that guide AI through complex tasks.

Prompt I use:

Project: LifeLens — a personal life-analysis tool that reads career, money, habits, and wellbeing as one connected system, built on behavioral-science research rather than horoscope-style guesses.

Build prompt (what you'd feed Claude to actually build the LifeLens tool):

Build "LifeLens" — a personal life-analysis web app with 6 sections: 
(1) an intake flow collecting identity/life-stage, career goals, money habits, daily routines, stress/energy levels, and top 3 concerns; 
(2) a Clarity Dashboard showing four KPIs — Career Momentum, Savings Rate, Habit Streak, and Wellbeing Pulse — computed from the intake and daily check-ins; 
(3) a Reflection Portal for a short daily/weekly check-in; 
(4) an AI-generated Insight Report (10 sections: Life Snapshot, Pattern Map, Career Path Fit, Money Behavior, Habit Diagnostics, Stress Drivers, 90-Day Plan, etc.) generated by Claude from the intake data;
(5) a habit tracker with streaks;
(6) a simple expense/savings log. Every insight the AI generates must cite the behavioral-science or financial-literacy research it's drawing on — no invented statistics. Use React with Tailwind, store data in local state (no localStorage), and keep the interface calm and non-judgmental — this is a reflection tool, not a scorecard.
